**Xuanzang**: 602 &#8211; 664 CE was an enourmously influential Chinese Buddhist monk, traveler, scholar and translator.

From Wikipedia: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang>

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
  <p>
    <strong>Xuanzang</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language">Chinese</a>: <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%8E%84%E5%A5%98">玄奘</a>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles">Wade–Giles</a>: <em>Hsüen Tsang</em>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Mandarin">[ɕɥɛ̌n.tsâŋ]</a>; 6 April 602 – 5 February 664), born <strong>Chen Hui</strong> or <strong>Chen Yi</strong> (<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%99%B3">陳</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%A4%98">褘</a> / <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%99%B3">陳</a><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%A6%95">禕</a>), also known by his Sanskrit <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma_name">Dharma name</a> <strong>Mokṣadeva</strong>,<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang#cite_note-:2-2">[1]</a></sup> was a 7th-century Chinese <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikkhu">Buddhist monk</a>, scholar, traveler, and translator. He is known for the epoch-making contributions to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism">Chinese Buddhism</a>, the travelogue of his journey to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent">India</a> in 629–645, his efforts to bring at least 657 Indian texts to China, and his translations of some of these texts.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang#cite_note-rongxi-3">[2]</a></sup> He was only able to translate 75 distinct sections of a total of 1335 chapters, but his translations included some of the most important <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana">Mahayana</a> scriptures.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang#cite_note-:2-2">[1]</a></sup>
  </p>
