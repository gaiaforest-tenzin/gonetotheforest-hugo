Welcome. This is the personal website of Ven. Ngawang Tenzin (Dr. Alex Anderson). Here I share information about [upcoming and past retreats, talks and other events](/posts/events/).  You will also find a small and searchable library of [resources](/posts/resources/), such as [audio](/posts/resources/audio/) and [video](/posts/resources/video/) recordings, [links](/posts/resources/links/), [suggested readings](/posts/resources/suggested-reading/), and downloadable [text resources](/posts/resources/text/) (chanting, glossaries etc.).  Here you will also find a summary of my journey so far, my practice and study interests, and a way to [contact me](/contact/) (if I don't reply quickly, its because I have "gone to the forest" ;).  For information about our project to build space for mindfulness, meditation and nature connection in the mid-north coast hinterland of NSW, Australia, please visit the [Gaia Forest website](https://www.gaiaforest.org)
### A note about the site title:

"Gone to the forest" is a translation of the Pali "*araññagato*".  This word appears in the introduction to both [ānāpānassatisutta](https://suttacentral.net/mn118/en/sujato?lang=en&layout=linebyline&reference=main%2Cpts&notes=none&highlight=false&script=latin) and the [satipaṭṭhānasutta](https://suttacentral.net/mn10/en/sujato?layout=sidebyside&reference=none&notes=asterisk&highlight=false&script=latin), in the phrase "*araññagato vā, rukkhamūlagato vā, suññāgāragato vā*"  - "gone to a wilderness, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut" - instructions related to finding suitable conditions for the cultivation of mindfulness. It has also been my life-long tendency.

For a little more about my journey so far, please see my [Bio page](/bio/), or for more detail - this  [CV](/cv/).
