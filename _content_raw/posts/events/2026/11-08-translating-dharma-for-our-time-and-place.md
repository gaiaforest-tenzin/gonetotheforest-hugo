In this talk for the Indonesian Buddhist Society, I revisited the theme of [Silk Road Dharma](/posts/events/2026/11-03-silk-road-dharma-buddhist-library/) from my talk at the Buddhist Library a few days earlier — this time bringing in the Silk Road's often-overlooked maritime route, prepared specifically for this audience. I spoke about the Tang-dynasty monk Yijing, who travelled by sea through the Sunda and Malacca Straits in the seventh century and found the Buddhist kingdom of Śrīvijaya, in what is now Indonesia, such an important centre of learning that he recommended fellow Chinese monks study there before continuing on to Nālandā. From there I returned to *mettā* (loving-kindness) as a case study in translation — including how *karuṇā* (compassion) and *mettā* itself (*cinta kasih* in Indonesian) carry into the Indonesian language — before closing with a guided *mettā* meditation.
