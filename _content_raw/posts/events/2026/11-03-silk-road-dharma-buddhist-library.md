In this talk for the Buddhist Library, at their new home in Glebe, we explored "Silk Road Dharma" — using Buddhism's historical journey along the Silk Road as a lens on how we translate the Dharma into our own time and place. We trace the Buddha's image from its earliest aniconic centuries (an empty chair, a footprint, a stūpa — no statues at all) through the Gandhāran period, when Greek sculptural influence gave the Buddha his familiar robes and curled hair, and followed translator-monks like Xuanzang and Kumārajīva as the Dharma moved into China — from early "matching concepts" (*géyì*) translation to the more careful work of finding terms, like *kōng* for *śūnyatā*, that actually fit. Taking *mettā* (loving-kindness) as a case study in this ongoing work of translation, I proposed "kinship" as an English rendering, and explored its resonance with the Christian notion of *agápē* and with the neuroscience of oxytocin and in-group bonding, before closing with a guided *mettā bhāvanā*.
