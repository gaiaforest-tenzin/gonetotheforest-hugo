~ 400 CE. Attributed to Asanga (300-350 BCE) and/or Maitreya

From Wikipedia:

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
  <p>
    The <em>Yogācārabhūmi</em> is generally associated with the Indian Yogācāra school because it contains certain unique Yogācāra doctrines, like the <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Consciousnesses">eight consciousnesses</a> and the <em>ālaya-vijñāna</em> (storehouse or foundational consciousness)<em>.</em> According to Ulrich Timme Kragh, &#8220;its overall objective seems to be to present a coherent structure of Buddhist yoga practice with the Mahāyāna path of the <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva">bodhisattva</a> placed at the pinnacle of the system&#8221;, but substantial parts also deal with non-Mahāyāna &#8220;<a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikaya_Buddhism">mainstream</a>&#8221; practices.<sup><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra#cite_note-:02-1">[1]</a></sup> The text also shows strong affinity to the <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidharma">Abhidharma</a> works of the Mainstream Buddhist <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarvastivada">Sarvāstivāda</a> school, adopting many of its technical terminology and classifications of phenomena (<em><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmas">dharmas</a></em>).<sup><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra#cite_note-3">[3]</a></sup>
  </p>
  
  <p>
    While it likely contains earlier materials, the YBh is thought to have reached its final redaction in the fourth century CE.<sup><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra#cite_note-4">[4]</a></sup> Traditional sources name either the Indian thinker <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asanga">Asaṅga</a> (ca. 300-350) or the <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva">bodhisattva</a> <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitreya">Maitreya</a> as author, but most modern scholars hold that it is a composite text with different chronological textual layers and various authors, though this does not rule out the possibility that <a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asanga">Asaṅga</a> was among them.<sup><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup><sup><a href="https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yog%C4%81c%C4%81rabh%C5%ABmi-%C5%9A%C4%81stra#cite_note-6">[6]</a></sup>
  </p>
</blockquote>
