From wikipedia:

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
  <p>
    <strong>Vasubandhu</strong> (<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters">traditional Chinese</a>: 世親; ; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin">pinyin</a>: <em>Shìqīn</em>; Tibetan: དབྱིག་གཉེན་ <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wylie_transliteration">Wylie</a>: <em>dbyig gnyen</em>; <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floruit">fl.</a> 4th to 5th century CE) was an influential Indian <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikkhu">Buddhist monk</a> and scholar.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu#cite_note-Brill-1">[1]</a></sup> He was a philosopher who wrote commentary on the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidharma">Abhidharma</a>, from the perspectives of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarvastivada">Sarvastivada</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sautr%C4%81ntika">Sautrāntika</a> schools. After his conversion to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana">Mahayana Buddhism</a>, along with his half-<sup>[<em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed">citation needed</a></em>]</sup>brother, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asanga">Asanga</a>, he was also one of the main founders of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogacara">Yogacara</a> school.
  </p>
  
  <p>
    Vasubandhu&#8217;s <em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhidharmako%C5%9Bak%C4%81rik%C4%81">Abhidharmakośakārikā</a></em> (&#8220;Commentary on the Treasury of the Abhidharma&#8221;) is widely used in Tibetan and East Asian Buddhism, as the major source for non-Mahayana Abhidharma philosophy. His philosophical verse works set forth the standard for the Indian Yogacara metaphysics of &#8220;appearance only&#8221; (<em>vijñapti-mātra</em>), which has been described as a form of &#8220;<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemological_idealism">epistemological idealism</a>&#8220;, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_(philosophy)">phenomenology</a><sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu#cite_note-2">[2]</a></sup> and close to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant">Immanuel Kant</a>&#8216;s <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_idealism">transcendental idealism</a>.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu#cite_note-Gold-3">[3]</a></sup> Apart from this, he wrote several commentaries, works on logic, argumentation and devotional poetry.
  </p>
  
  <p>
    Vasubandhu is one of the most influential thinkers in the Indian Buddhist philosophical tradition. Because of their association with <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda">Nalanda</a> university, Vasubandhu and Asanga are amongst the so-called Seventeen Nalanda Masters.<sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu#cite_note-KumarDriem2020-4">[4]</a></sup><sup><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasubandhu#cite_note-5">[5]</a></sup> In <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Ddo_Shinsh%C5%AB">Jōdo Shinshū</a>, he is considered the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineage_(Buddhism)">Second Patriarch</a>; in <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Buddhism">Chan Buddhism</a>, he is the 21st Patriarch.
  </p>
</blockquote>
